15 Best Korean Melodramas of All Time: Heartbreaking K-Dramas That Will Make You Cry

Published: November 13, 2026 · Updated: November 13, 2026

Some Korean dramas make us smile, while others stay in our hearts long after the final episode. The most unforgettable melodramas often do both: they introduce beautiful relationships, create moments of hope, and then remind us how complicated love and life can be.

If you are looking for emotional stories, heartbreaking romances, family struggles, or characters who face impossible choices, Korean melodramas offer plenty of memorable experiences. From classic television hits of the early 2000s to more recent stories, these dramas have touched audiences across generations.

Here are 15 Korean melodramas worth discovering. This is an editorial selection, not an official ranking. The list includes tragic romances, historical stories, family dramas, and emotional human stories. Some titles are especially famous for their sad endings, so prepare yourself for a few tears.

1. Autumn in My Heart (2000)

Autumn in My Heart is one of the classic Korean melodramas that helped introduce emotional Korean storytelling to international audiences. The story follows Yoon Eun-suh and Yoon Joon-suh, whose lives change dramatically after a family secret reveals that they were switched at birth.

As the characters grow older, their relationship becomes increasingly complicated by family expectations, separation, and illness. The drama is remembered for its emotional performances, beautiful scenery, and tragic love story.

2. Winter Sonata (2002)

Winter Sonata is a landmark romance about first love, memory, and the pain of losing someone important. Yoo-jin believes her first love, Joon-sang, is gone from her life forever. Years later, she meets a man who looks remarkably like him.

The reunion brings back old feelings while raising questions about identity and the past. Its winter landscapes, memorable soundtrack, and emotional atmosphere helped make it one of the most influential Korean romance dramas of its era.

3. Stairway to Heaven (2003)

For viewers who enjoy classic tearjerkers, Stairway to Heaven is a memorable title. It follows childhood friends Cha Song-joo and Han Jung-seo, whose love is repeatedly tested by family conflict, separation, jealousy, and devastating circumstances.

The series is famous for its intense melodramatic storytelling and emotional performances. Its themes of loyalty and enduring love made it one of the best-known tearjerker dramas of the early Hallyu era.

4. I'm Sorry, I Love You (2004)

I'm Sorry, I Love You tells the story of Cha Moo-hyuk, a man whose difficult past shapes his view of the world. After returning to Korea, he becomes connected to Song Eun-chae, a woman whose kindness gradually changes his life.

This drama explores loneliness, family, identity, and love that arrives under painful circumstances. It is often remembered for its emotionally heavy atmosphere and its willingness to tell a heartbreaking story.

5. What Happened in Bali (2004)

What Happened in Bali is a romance melodrama involving complicated relationships, wealth, ambition, and emotional conflict. The story begins with people whose paths cross during a trip to Bali, but their connections become much more complicated after they return to Korea.

Rather than presenting a simple love story, the series explores jealousy, class differences, obsession, and the consequences of difficult choices. It is a classic for viewers who enjoy intense and unpredictable melodrama.

♡ CewekPedia Tips

If you are new to older Korean melodramas, start with Winter Sonata or Autumn in My Heart. Both are important examples of the emotional storytelling that helped Korean dramas gain international popularity. Keep in mind that older dramas may have a slower pace than many modern series.

6. Moon Lovers: Scarlet Heart Ryeo (2016)

Moon Lovers: Scarlet Heart Ryeo combines historical drama, time travel, palace politics, and tragic romance. A modern woman named Go Ha-jin finds herself transported to the Goryeo era, where she becomes involved in the lives and rivalries of royal princes.

Her relationship with Wang So develops in a world filled with ambition, loyalty, and political danger. The drama is especially remembered for its emotional ending and the difficult choices faced by its characters.

7. Uncontrollably Fond (2016)

Uncontrollably Fond follows Shin Joon-young, a famous actor and singer, who reunites with documentary producer Noh Eul. Their relationship is connected to painful memories from the past, while new challenges threaten their future.

The series explores regret, second chances, illness, and the meaning of time. It is a strong choice for viewers who enjoy emotional romance stories with a bittersweet atmosphere.

8. The Smile Has Left Your Eyes (2018)

The Smile Has Left Your Eyes is a dark romantic mystery centered on Kim Moo-young, a mysterious man whose past is difficult to understand, and Yoo Jin-kang, a woman who becomes emotionally connected to him.

As their relationship develops, secrets and painful events begin to emerge. The drama blends romance, suspense, and psychological tension, creating a story that feels very different from a conventional love story.

9. Mr. Sunshine (2018)

Set during a turbulent period in Korean history, Mr. Sunshine tells the story of Eugene Choi, a man who leaves Korea as a child and later returns as a United States Marine officer. He meets Go Ae-shin, a noblewoman secretly involved in the struggle for her country's independence.

Their relationship develops against a backdrop of political change, sacrifice, and uncertainty. The series is known for its cinematic presentation, strong performances, and emotional portrayal of people living through a difficult historical era.

10. The Hymn of Death (2018)

The Hymn of Death is a short historical romance inspired by the real-life story of soprano Yun Sim-deok and playwright Kim Woo-jin. Set during the Japanese colonial period, it follows two people who develop feelings for each other despite social expectations and personal responsibilities.

With its restrained storytelling and tragic atmosphere, this series offers a different kind of emotional experience. It is a suitable choice for viewers who prefer a short drama rather than a long television series.

11. My Mister (2018)

Not every heartbreaking Korean drama is about romance. My Mister tells the story of Park Dong-hoon, an engineer dealing with personal and professional difficulties, and Lee Ji-an, a young woman carrying heavy responsibilities and emotional wounds.

Their unexpected connection gradually becomes a source of understanding and comfort. The drama explores loneliness, hardship, kindness, and the quiet strength people can find in one another.

12. The Red Sleeve (2021–2022)

The Red Sleeve is a historical romance inspired by the relationship between Crown Prince Yi San, who later becomes King Jeongjo, and court lady Seong Deok-im.

Their feelings develop within the strict rules of palace life. The story explores the tension between personal happiness and royal duty, showing how love can become complicated when individual choices are limited by responsibility and tradition.

13. Youth of May (2021)

Youth of May is set in Gwangju in 1980, during a significant and painful period in South Korean history. It follows medical student Hwang Hee-tae and nurse Kim Myung-hee as they fall in love amid political unrest and uncertainty.

Their relationship is filled with hope, but the historical circumstances surrounding them make their future uncertain. The drama is especially moving because it connects a personal love story with real historical events.

14. Move to Heaven (2021)

Move to Heaven is an emotional human drama about Geu-ru, a young man with autism who works with his father in a special cleaning business. After a family tragedy, he begins working with his uncle Sang-gu.

Together, they organize the belongings left behind by people who have passed away. Each episode reveals memories, relationships, and stories that might otherwise remain unseen. It is a touching series about grief, compassion, and the importance of understanding other people.

15. Twenty-Five Twenty-One (2022)

Twenty-Five Twenty-One is a coming-of-age romance set around the late 1990s financial crisis in South Korea. It follows Na Hee-do, a determined young fencer, and Baek Yi-jin, a young man whose family faces financial difficulties.

Their friendship and relationship develop as they pursue their dreams and face the realities of growing up. The drama captures the excitement of youth, ambition, first love, and the painful realization that not every relationship ends the way we once imagined.

Which Korean Melodrama Should You Watch First?

With so many emotional dramas to choose from, the best starting point depends on the kind of story you enjoy.

  • For classic tragic romance: Autumn in My Heart, Winter Sonata, or Stairway to Heaven.
  • For intense and heartbreaking love stories: I'm Sorry, I Love You or Uncontrollably Fond.
  • For historical romance: Moon Lovers: Scarlet Heart Ryeo or The Red Sleeve.
  • For emotional stories based on historical settings: Mr. Sunshine or Youth of May.
  • For a moving story beyond romance: My Mister or Move to Heaven.
  • For a modern coming-of-age story: Twenty-Five Twenty-One.

Why Are Korean Melodramas So Emotional?

Korean melodramas often focus on relationships under pressure. Characters may face family expectations, illness, social inequality, historical events, personal sacrifice, or the consequences of decisions made long ago.

Another reason these stories connect with viewers is their attention to everyday emotions. Small gestures, quiet conversations, family memories, and moments of separation can become just as powerful as major plot twists.

The genre has also changed over time. Older classics often use traditional melodramatic storytelling, while newer productions may combine emotional romance with history, social themes, mystery, or coming-of-age stories.

Final Thoughts

The best Korean melodramas are not simply stories designed to make viewers cry. They remind us of love, memory, family, sacrifice, and the difficult moments that shape people's lives.

Whether you choose the classic romance of Winter Sonata, the historical tragedy of Moon Lovers: Scarlet Heart Ryeo, the emotional depth of My Mister, or the bittersweet youth story of Twenty-Five Twenty-One, each drama offers a different reason to remember its characters.

Sometimes, a great drama does not need a perfect ending. It only needs a story that stays with us long after the screen goes dark.
